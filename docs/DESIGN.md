# World and rules

The weather stations once served settlements. Their central prediction loop became unable to end a day. Machines now interpret change as a fault. The player repairs the world by restoring the five circuits, not by serving an arbitrary faction or killing a conventional fantasy monster roster.

Water, wind, heat, memory and storms form the campaign structure. Each circuit is restored by completing any eligible portal of its corresponding tier. Main signals are discoverable navigation anchors; they do not exclude exploration or alternate portals.

Three independent progression channels coexist: XP from finite combat encounters; equipment from the keeper reward and overworld finds; one permanent improvement chosen after portal completion. Equipment needs explicit equipping in the overworld. Repeated low-tier runs have bounded rank pools and diminishing XP usefulness. Mastered pools yield limited-use expedition reserves instead of endlessly compounding a cheap permanent stat.

Death preserves earned XP and established progression. The unfinished expedition reward is not granted. Starting or abandoning an expedition does not roll back unrelated progression. Main quest completion opens a final portal near home, followed by an epilogue and optional continued exploration.

The game does not promise victory in every battle. Its hard guarantee is a finite, reachable and completable generated plan, backed by validation and fallback construction. A player may knowingly enter a harder portal and lose. The Explorer starting mode reduces incoming damage while retaining portal tiers and the campaign structure.

The world is deliberately finite and compact. It is a complete single-player campaign with repeatable generated expeditions, not a live service, MMO or literal infinite universe.
